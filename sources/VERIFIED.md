# Verified quotations and figures

Quotations and figures checked **against a primary source held in this
directory** (or read directly at a stable link, recorded here), with the
wording, numbers and location as actually printed. Once an item is here, the
manuscript can use it without a `[[?...]]` marker.

**Nothing goes in this file on the strength of recall, a secondary summary, or
a search-result snippet.** A figure quoted in a popular book is checked against
the paper it came from; a sutta quoted from a translation names the translator.

This file *is* committed — it records the verification, not the copyrighted work.

Format:

    ## Autor (ano), *Título*

    Source: `arquivo-em-sources.pdf` (or the link read), and where in it.

    > The exact wording, or the exact figures.

    Used in: capítulo "Título".

## Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (SN 56.11), trad. Bhikkhu Sujato

Source: `sujato-sn56.11-en.md` (SuttaCentral, CC0), segmentos 4.1–4.10 (as
quatro verdades), 5.1–5.12 (as doze tarefas: compreender, abandonar, realizar,
desenvolver). Português de Íris Gradim, nota ao pé do capítulo.

> Now this is the noble truth of suffering. Rebirth is suffering; old age is suffering; illness is suffering; death is suffering; being coupled with the disliked is suffering; separation from the liked is suffering; not getting what you wish for is suffering. In brief, the five grasping aggregates are suffering. (4.1–4.2)

> It's the craving that leads to future lives, mixed up with relishing and greed, taking pleasure there wherever it alights. That is, craving for sensual pleasures, craving for continued existence, and craving for nonexistence. (4.4–4.5)

Used in: capítulo "As Quatro Nobres Verdades como diagnóstico".

## Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta (SN 22.59), trad. Bhikkhu Sujato

Source: `sujato-sn22.59-en.md` (SuttaCentral, CC0), segmentos 2.1–2.5
(argumento do controle), 6.1–6.8 (argumento da impermanência), 7.1, 11.1–11.2,
12.3. Páli conferido no root_text da API (rūpaṁ … anattā; niccaṁ vā aniccaṁ
vā; etaṁ mama, esohamasmi, eso me attā). Português de Íris Gradim.

> Mendicants, form is not-self. For if form were self, it wouldn't lead to affliction. And you could compel form: 'May my form be like this! May it not be like that!' But because form is not-self, it leads to affliction. And you can't compel form: 'May my form be like this! May it not be like that!' (2.1–2.5)

> "Is form permanent or impermanent?" "Impermanent, sir." "But if it's impermanent, is it suffering or happiness?" "Suffering, sir." "But if it's impermanent, suffering, and perishable, is it fit to be regarded thus: 'This is mine, I am this, this is my self'?" "No, sir." (6.2–6.8)

> past, future, or present; internal or external; solid or subtle … 'This is not mine, I am not this, this is not my self.' (7.1)

Sujato traduz saṅkhārā como "choices". Mendis (Access to Insight,
sn22.059.mend) traduz "mental formations".

Used in: capítulo "Impermanência e não-eu".

## Sharf (2015), "Is mindfulness Buddhist? (and why it matters)"

Source: `sharf-2015-is-mindfulness-buddhist-en.pdf` (cópia de autor, Berkeley,
via web.archive.org), *Transcultural Psychiatry* 52(4): 470–484.

- Resumo (p. 470): "bare attention" remonta às reformas birmanesas da primeira
  metade do século XX; inovações semelhantes no Chan e no Dzogchen; acessíveis
  a leigos; "astonishingly quick results"; recebidas com "suspicion and criticism".
- p. 472: Buddhist modernism ("sometimes known as 'Protestant Buddhism'") como
  "rational, empirical, and therapeutically oriented tradition compatible with
  modern science"; linhagens de Ledi Sayadaw e Mingun Sayadaw.
- p. 473: Mahāsi Sayadaw (1904–1982); método pensado para leigos; "the
  cultivation of liberating insight did not require advanced skill in
  concentration (samatha) or the experience of absorption (jhāna)"; não
  exigia abhidhamma nem renúncia; retiro curto; "easy to export"; Nyanaponika
  Thera (Siegmund Feniger, 1901–1994), "German born", aluno de Mahāsi, "coined
  the term 'bare attention'" no livro de 1954 *The Heart of Buddhist Meditation*.
- pp. 473–474: smṛti/sati "originally meant 'to remember'"; definição canônica
  citada: "he is one who remembers, who recollects what was done and said long
  before"; Gethin (1992): sati envolve discriminar o valor moral; "there is
  little 'bare' about the faculty of sati".
- p. 477: "what is missing in the modern mindfulness movement is precisely this
  gradual transformation, which involves active engagement with Buddhist
  doctrine and Buddhist 'forms of life'".
- p. 478: o samgha antigo como comunidade renunciante, "communal, celibate,
  and highly regulated lifestyle".

Used in: capítulos "Quem foi o Buda — e quem ele não foi" e "O caminho, e onde a meditação entra nele".
