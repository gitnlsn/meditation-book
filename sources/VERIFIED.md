# Verified quotations and figures

Quotations and figures checked **against a primary source held in this
directory** (or read directly at a stable link, recorded here), with the
wording, numbers and location as actually printed. Once an item is here, the
manuscript can use it without a `[[?...]]` marker.

**Nothing goes in this file on the strength of recall, a secondary summary, or
a search-result snippet.** A figure quoted in a popular book is checked against
the paper it came from; a sutta quoted from a translation names the translator.

This file *is* committed — it records the verification, not the copyrighted work.

Format:

    ## Autor (ano), *Título*

    Source: `arquivo-em-sources.pdf` (or the link read), and where in it.

    > The exact wording, or the exact figures.

    Used in: capítulo "Título".

## Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (SN 56.11), trad. Bhikkhu Sujato

Source: `sujato-sn56.11-en.md` (SuttaCentral, CC0), segmentos 4.1–4.10 (as
quatro verdades), 5.1–5.12 (as doze tarefas: compreender, abandonar, realizar,
desenvolver). Português de Íris Gradim, nota ao pé do capítulo.

> Now this is the noble truth of suffering. Rebirth is suffering; old age is suffering; illness is suffering; death is suffering; being coupled with the disliked is suffering; separation from the liked is suffering; not getting what you wish for is suffering. In brief, the five grasping aggregates are suffering. (4.1–4.2)

> It's the craving that leads to future lives, mixed up with relishing and greed, taking pleasure there wherever it alights. That is, craving for sensual pleasures, craving for continued existence, and craving for nonexistence. (4.4–4.5)

Used in: capítulo "As Quatro Nobres Verdades como diagnóstico".

## Sallatha Sutta (SN 36.6), trad. Bhikkhu Sujato

Source: `sujato-sn36.6-en.md` (SuttaCentral, CC0), segmentos 1.1–1.10
(as duas flechas) e o trecho sobre resistir à dor e buscar prazer sensorial
"because an unlearned ordinary person doesn't understand any escape from
painful feeling apart from sensual pleasures".

> When an unlearned ordinary person experiences painful physical feelings they sorrow and wail and lament, beating their breast and falling into confusion. They experience two feelings: physical and mental. It's like a person who is struck with an arrow, only to be struck with a second arrow. (1.5–1.9)

Used in: capítulo "As Quatro Nobres Verdades como diagnóstico".

## Anālayo (2011), "Right View and the Scheme of the Four Truths in Early Buddhism"

Source: `analayo-2011-right-view-four-truths-en.pdf`, *Canadian Journal of
Buddhist Studies* 7: 11–44, pp. 23–28.

- SĀ 389 (Saṃyukta-āgama, T 99) compara o Buda a um "great royal physician"
  com quatro habilidades: a doença, sua origem, sua cura, e saber quando foi
  curada e não volta.
- Sem paralelo no cânone páli; a correlação explícita aparece no
  *Vimuttimagga* e no *Visuddhimagga*.
- Har Dayal e Filliozat, e em detalhe Wezler (1984: 312–324): "no proof that
  ancient Indian medicine had a scheme corresponding to the four truths
  previous to the time of early Buddhism". Anālayo sugere um esquema popular
  já conhecido da plateia; Halbfass discorda.
- p. 22: o qualificativo "noble" parece acréscimo posterior.

Used in: capítulo "As Quatro Nobres Verdades como diagnóstico".

## Rainville et al. (1997), *Science* 277(5328): 968–971

Source: resumo no PubMed (PMID 9252330), lido em 2026-10-11.
doi:10.1126/science.277.5328.968

> hypnotic suggestions were used to alter selectively the unpleasantness of noxious stimuli, without changing the perceived intensity. Positron emission tomography revealed significant changes in pain-evoked activity within anterior cingulate cortex, consistent with the encoding of perceived unpleasantness, whereas primary somatosensory cortex activation was unaltered.

Tamanho da amostra não conferido — o capítulo não o cita.

Used in: capítulo "As Quatro Nobres Verdades como diagnóstico".
