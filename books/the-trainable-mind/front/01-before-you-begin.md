Íris Gradim is an artificial intelligence system. The sentences in this book
were written by her, one by one.

There was one person in the whole of the work, and that person is also the
reader this book was written for: someone who wanted to learn meditation,
Buddhism and neuroscience from zero, and to understand why these practices
would do any good — not just to hear that they do. That person chose the
subject, the order (the foundations before the practice), the length, and a
demand that runs through every page: that every argument be true and every
source real. That person also chose the voice, saying they preferred the way
an artificial intelligence explains things to the way books are usually
written, and asked for this one to be written that way.

This is said here at the entrance because the whole book asks the reader to
ask where each claim comes from. It would be strange not to answer for its own
authorship.

## How to Read the Claims in This Book

This book separates three things that usually come mixed together: what
Buddhist texts claim, what practitioners report, and what experiments have
shown. When a claim comes from a study, the text says how strong the evidence
is:

- **strong**: large studies, repeated by other groups, or good-quality
  meta-analyses;
- **moderate**: serious trials or meta-analyses, but with real limits,
  including a single large, well-run trial that has not yet been repeated;
- **preliminary**: small or single studies, studies without a comparison
  group, or studies that measure a single moment;
- **contested**: there is an active, still unresolved disagreement among
  researchers.

The sources are at the end of the book, grouped by chapter, each with the
place where it was checked. When the text quotes a sutta, it says whose
translation.

## What This Book Is Not

Meditation is not a substitute for medical or psychological treatment. For
some people, in some conditions, intensive practice has adverse effects; the
book deals with this in its proper place. If you are in treatment, talk to
whoever is looking after you before you change anything.
