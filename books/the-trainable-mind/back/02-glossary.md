The terms the book defines, in the sense the book uses them, with the
chapter where each one is explained in full. Pali terms keep the spelling
the text uses and come, first of all, with what they mean.

## A

**active control**
:   A control group that does another activity with the same length, the same format and the same human warmth, but without the ingredient being tested. Even it only neutralizes the placebo if both groups expect to improve to the same degree.\
    → chapter {{cap:como-saber-se-algo-funciona}}

**adverse effect**
:   Any unwanted effect associated with a treatment or practice. In meditation, it is kept separate from expected discomfort and assessed on five points: whether it occurred, whether it is linked to the practice, how long it lasted, whether it felt good or bad, and whether it impaired the person's functioning.\
    → chapter {{cap:quando-a-meditacao-da-errado}}

**allostatic load**
:   The accumulated wear and tear when stress responses are triggered too often or fail to switch off once the threat has passed. Its protective side, allostasis, is the body's ability to maintain stability *by changing*.\
    → chapter {{cap:estresse-o-alarme-que-nao-desliga}}

**amygdala**
:   A small brain region linked to detecting threat and emotional relevance. It also takes part in novelty and surprise, which is why seeing it "light up" in a scan is not enough to conclude that the person felt fear.\
    → chapter {{cap:como-saber-se-algo-funciona}}

**ānāpānasati**
:   "Attention (*sati*) to breathing in and breathing out": the practice of attention to the breath described in the *Ānāpānasati Sutta* (MN 118). It asks you to *know* how the breath is, without controlling it, as the first step of a sixteen-stage path that, in the tradition, aims at liberation.\
    → chapter {{cap:atencao-na-respiracao}}

**anattā**
:   "Not-self": the negation of *attā*, "self." The *Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta* argues that none of the five aggregates is the self, because none is under our command and all are impermanent. The doctrine denies a fixed, independent self, not that the person exists.\
    → chapter {{cap:impermanencia-e-nao-eu}}

**anchor**
:   In the modern vocabulary of meditation, the object attention returns to whenever it drifts off. The breath is the classic choice, but the anchor can be a sound, the feet on the floor or the hands in the lap.\
    → chapter {{cap:atencao-na-respiracao}}

**anicca**
:   "Impermanent": what has a beginning, a duration and an end. In the early suttas, it means that nothing lasts indefinitely; the idea that each phenomenon lasts a single instant is a later development.\
    → chapter {{cap:impermanencia-e-nao-eu}}

**attentional blink**
:   A well-known limit of human attention: when two targets appear very close together in a rapid stream of images, the second often goes unnoticed, as if attention "blinked" after catching the first.\
    → chapter {{cap:atencao-emocao-e-habito}}

**autonomic nervous system**
:   The part of the nervous system that regulates, without your deciding, the heart, blood vessels, breathing, digestion and glands. It has a sympathetic branch, the accelerator, and a parasympathetic branch, the brake, whose main nerve is the vagus.\
    → chapter {{cap:estresse-o-alarme-que-nao-desliga}}

## B

**bare attention**
:   An expression coined by the monk Nyanaponika Thera in 1954 for a neutral, nonjudgmental attention. According to the historian Robert Sharf, there is little that is "bare" in the *sati* of the early texts, which involves discerning the moral value of what arises.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-caminho-e-onde-a-meditacao-entra-nele}}

**bodhisattva**
:   In the Mahāyāna, someone who follows the gradual path that runs from the aspiration to awakening to its realization.\
    → chapter {{cap:quem-foi-o-buda-e-quem-ele-nao-foi}}

**body scan**
:   A practice in which attention moves slowly through the body, part by part, noticing what is there without trying to change anything. It comes from MBSR, which adapted contemplations of the body found in the tradition for a clinical goal.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-corpo-o-sono-e-a-dor}}

**brahmavihāras**
:   "Divine abodes" or "sublime abodes": four qualities the tradition treats as trainable, with a method and an order — *mettā*, *karuṇā*, *muditā* and *upekkhā*. In the tradition, they belong to an ethical and religious path, not to a wellness technique.\
    → chapter {{cap:bondade-e-compaixao-como-treino}}

**Buddha**
:   A title, not a name: it means "awakened," someone who found the path to the end of suffering and taught it to others. The historical Buddha, Gautama (in Pali, Gotama), lived in northern India; his dates are contested.\
    → chapter {{cap:quem-foi-o-buda-e-quem-ele-nao-foi}}

**Buddhist exceptionalism**
:   The philosopher Evan Thompson's name for the belief that Buddhism is superior to other religions because it is inherently rational and empirical, or that it is not a religion at all but a "science of the mind" or a therapy.\
    → chapter {{cap:quem-foi-o-buda-e-quem-ele-nao-foi}}

**Buddhist modernism**
:   The forms of Buddhism that arose from the mid-nineteenth century on, in the encounter between Asia and the West, built around the notion that Buddhism is a rational, empirical, therapy-oriented tradition compatible with science. Asian reformers were among its protagonists.\
    → chapter {{cap:quem-foi-o-buda-e-quem-ele-nao-foi}}

## C

**CBT-I**
:   Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia: a structured treatment of a few weeks that works on the habits and beliefs that keep insomnia going. It is the first-choice treatment for chronic insomnia in adults.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-corpo-o-sono-e-a-dor}}

**chronic pain**
:   Pain that persists for more than three months, by the criterion proposed for the international classification of diseases (ICD-11). It is not acute pain that has lasted too long: it is a condition with a life of its own.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-corpo-o-sono-e-a-dor}}

**compassion**
:   In the sense research uses, a feeling of care in the face of another's suffering, together with the motivation to help. It does not require feeling the same pain, and it includes warmth, not only distress.\
    → chapter {{cap:bondade-e-compaixao-como-treino}}

**construction of the self**
:   The idea that the sense of having a body, of being in a place, of being the author of your own actions and of having a history is put together continuously by the brain from several sources, and can be taken apart or fooled under special conditions. Constructed does not mean false.\
    → chapter {{cap:impermanencia-e-nao-eu}}

**control group**
:   A group of similar people who do *not* receive the practice being tested and are measured at the same points. Only the difference between the groups counts.\
    → chapter {{cap:como-saber-se-algo-funciona}}

**cortex**
:   The outer layer of the brain. Its thickness is one of the measures of brain structure in which the largest trial of MBSR found no change after eight weeks.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-que-a-ciencia-da-meditacao-mostra-e-o-que}}

**cortisol**
:   The main stress hormone in human beings, released by the adrenal glands at the end of the HPA axis. It mobilizes energy, adjusts the immune system and, under normal conditions, helps switch off the alarm itself.\
    → chapter {{cap:estresse-o-alarme-que-nao-desliga}}

**counting**
:   Numbering the breaths, from one to ten, to give the mind a simple support. It is not in the sutta: it comes from the *Visuddhimagga*, which treats it as a temporary device, to be dropped once attention stays settled on the breath.\
    → chapter {{cap:atencao-na-respiracao}}

## D

**default mode network**
:   A group of brain regions, among them the medial prefrontal cortex and the posterior cingulate cortex, that tend to activate together when a person is not focused on an external task. Linking it to mind wandering is reasonable; treating it as "the ego network" is reverse inference.\
    → chapter {{cap:a-mente-que-divaga}}

**depersonalization**
:   A persistent sense of estrangement from yourself, as if you were watching your own life from outside. When the estrangement falls on the world around you, it is called derealization. For a few moments during practice it is not, in itself, a sign of trouble; the warning sign is when it persists after the session, frightens you and gets in the way of life.\
    → chapter {{cap:quando-a-meditacao-da-errado}}

**Dharma**
:   In Pali, *Dhamma*: the Buddha's teaching and, at the same time, the order of things that the teaching describes.\
    → chapter {{cap:quem-foi-o-buda-e-quem-ele-nao-foi}}

**dukkha**
:   Unsatisfactoriness: the background discomfort of living in a world where nothing that matters to us stands still. It is usually translated as "suffering," but it is wider than pain and includes pleasant experiences, because they too change and end.\
    → chapter {{cap:as-quatro-nobres-verdades-como-diagnostico}}

**dysregulated arousal**
:   Signs of hyperarousal (a state of excessive alertness) and of dissociation. In a careful study of an eight-week program, they were associated with the lasting bad effects a minority of participants had.\
    → chapter {{cap:quando-a-meditacao-da-errado}}

## E

**effect size**
:   How large the difference between groups is, not just whether it exists. The most common one, *d*, measures that difference against how much people already differ from one another: with *d* = 0.35, the person who practiced comes out ahead in about 60 of every 100 draws.\
    → chapter {{cap:como-saber-se-algo-funciona}}

**emotion regulation**
:   The set of processes by which a person influences which emotions they have, when, how intensely, and how they express them: suppressing, reappraising, or observing and allowing.\
    → chapter {{cap:atencao-emocao-e-habito}}

**empathic distress fatigue**
:   The exhaustion of someone who repeatedly feels other people's pain without a way of turning it into care, in the term proposed by Olga Klimecki and Tania Singer. It is what is usually called "compassion fatigue"; what wears people down, they argue, is unregulated empathy, not compassion.\
    → chapter {{cap:bondade-e-compaixao-como-treino}}

**empathy**
:   In the sense research uses, sharing another person's emotional state: feeling, to some degree, what they feel.\
    → chapter {{cap:bondade-e-compaixao-como-treino}}

**emptiness**
:   The claim that nothing has a nature of its own, independent of everything else (*svabhāva*). Within the Mahāyāna, the Madhyamaka school extended the not-self argument to all things; it is one school's reading, not all of Buddhism's.\
    → chapters {{cap:quem-foi-o-buda-e-quem-ele-nao-foi}} and {{cap:impermanencia-e-nao-eu}}

**equanimity**
:   The capacity to let an experience be present without pulling it in or pushing it away. It is not indifference: the equanimous person perceives clearly but does not add the second arrow.\
    → chapter {{cap:observar-sem-agarrar}}

**expected discomfort**
:   Boredom, restlessness, a sore knee, sleepiness, the discovery that the mind does not stop, avoided emotions that show up: what is part of learning to meditate and is not, in itself, a sign that something has gone wrong. It has to be kept separate from an adverse effect.\
    → chapter {{cap:quando-a-meditacao-da-errado}}

## F

**five aggregates**
:   In Pali, *khandha*: the Buddhist analysis of what makes up a person, in five groups of processes — form (*rūpa*), feeling (*vedanā*), perception (*saññā*), formations (*saṅkhārā*) and consciousness (*viññāṇa*). On the most common reading, the list is complete: there is no sixth item left over that would be "the owner."\
    → chapter {{cap:impermanencia-e-nao-eu}}

**five hindrances**
:   States the tradition regards as obstacles to practice: sensual desire, ill will, dullness and drowsiness, restlessness and remorse, and doubt. The *Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta* includes them among the things the practitioner observes.\
    → chapters {{cap:o-caminho-e-onde-a-meditacao-entra-nele}} and {{cap:observar-sem-agarrar}}

**fMRI**
:   Functional magnetic resonance imaging: an MRI scanner programmed to record changes in blood oxygenation at each small point of the brain. It does not see cells at work but a blood signal that follows their activity; the signal is weak and demands careful statistics.\
    → chapter {{cap:como-saber-se-algo-funciona}}

**focused attention**
:   Voluntarily directing attention to a chosen object, such as the breath, and bringing it back every time it leaves. With open monitoring, it is one of the two basic styles of practice in the distinction that organizes much of the research.\
    → chapter {{cap:atencao-emocao-e-habito}}

**four foundations of mindfulness**
:   The four fields of experience to which, in the *Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta*, attention is directed: the body, feelings (*vedanā*), the mind, and *dhammas*, patterns the tradition considers decisive, such as the obstacles to practice.\
    → chapter {{cap:observar-sem-agarrar}}

**Four Noble Truths**
:   The central teaching of Buddhism: there is dukkha; dukkha has a cause, taṇhā; dukkha can cease; the path to its cessation is the Noble Eightfold Path. They have the structure of a diagnosis, and each truth comes with a task.\
    → chapter {{cap:as-quatro-nobres-verdades-como-diagnostico}}

## G

**gray matter**
:   The tissue that holds the cell bodies of neurons and a large share of the synapses, as distinct from white matter, made of the long extensions that connect distant regions. When a study says it "increased," it means the analysis software estimated a little more of this tissue in some region.\
    → chapter {{cap:neuroplasticidade-sem-exagero}}

## H

**habit**
:   A behavior that fires on its own in a given context, without a conscious decision. It is an association between context and response formed by repetition, and the time it takes to form varies widely: from 18 to 254 days in the most cited study.\
    → chapter {{cap:atencao-emocao-e-habito}}

**Hebbian learning**
:   The idea Donald Hebb proposed in 1949: when one neuron repeatedly helps make another fire, the connection between the two grows stronger. Connections used together are reinforced.\
    → chapter {{cap:neuroplasticidade-sem-exagero}}

**hippocampus**
:   A curved structure, one on each side of the brain, on the inner part of the temporal lobes. It is essential for forming new memories and for finding your way in space, and it is the region of the London taxi driver studies.\
    → chapter {{cap:neuroplasticidade-sem-exagero}}

**HPA axis**
:   The hormonal chain of command of stress, the alarm's slow route: the hypothalamus signals the pituitary, which signals the adrenal glands, which release cortisol.\
    → chapter {{cap:estresse-o-alarme-que-nao-desliga}}

## I

**insomnia**
:   Persistent difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early without being able to get back to sleep, with a cost the next day. One bad night is not insomnia.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-corpo-o-sono-e-a-dor}}

**interoception**
:   The perception of the body's internal state — heartbeat, breathing, muscle tension, stomach, temperature — largely unconscious.\
    → chapter {{cap:estresse-o-alarme-que-nao-desliga}}

## K

**karuṇā**
:   Compassion: the wish that a being be free from suffering, in the face of that suffering. One of the four *brahmavihāras*.\
    → chapter {{cap:bondade-e-compaixao-como-treino}}

## M

**magga**
:   "Path": the fourth of the Four Noble Truths, the Noble Eightfold Path. It does not say "believe"; it says "do."\
    → chapter {{cap:as-quatro-nobres-verdades-como-diagnostico}}

**Mahāyāna**
:   The branch of Buddhism that gathers a broad family of schools with their own scriptures, marked by the *bodhisattva* path and by emptiness. It predominates in China, Japan, South Korea and Vietnam, and is probably the largest of the three branches.\
    → chapter {{cap:quem-foi-o-buda-e-quem-ele-nao-foi}}

**MBCT**
:   Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy: it combines mindfulness practices of the MBSR kind with elements of cognitive therapy to keep depression from coming back. In the large trials, it is eight weekly group sessions.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-que-a-ciencia-da-meditacao-mostra-e-o-que}}

**MBSR**
:   Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction: a group course, now eight weeks long, created by Jon Kabat-Zinn for patients with chronic pain. It is a secular adaptation of Buddhist practices, deliberately taken out of its religious context.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-que-a-ciencia-da-meditacao-mostra-e-o-que}}

**meta-analysis**
:   A study of studies: it gathers all the research on a question, under rules set in advance, and combines the results into a single number. It carries more weight than any single study, but inherits the weaknesses of the studies it gathers.\
    → chapter {{cap:como-saber-se-algo-funciona}}

**meta-awareness**
:   The capacity to notice what your own mind is doing while it does it: not just thinking, but knowing you are thinking; not just wandering, but noticing that you have wandered.\
    → chapter {{cap:a-mente-que-divaga}}

**mettā**
:   Goodwill, friendliness, the wish that a being be well and happy. It is often translated "loving-kindness"; Sujato translates it as "love." One of the four *brahmavihāras*.\
    → chapter {{cap:bondade-e-compaixao-como-treino}}

**mind wandering**
:   When attention leaves what you are doing and goes to thoughts and feelings unrelated to the task, usually without your noticing. It took up almost half of the moments sampled in daily life, and it has costs (errors) and benefits (planning, creativity).\
    → chapter {{cap:a-mente-que-divaga}}

**mindfulness**
:   The English word used to translate *sati*. Today it also names a set of secular practices and programs; in Jon Kabat-Zinn's definition, it is paying attention on purpose, in the present moment and without judgment. Whether it is a good translation of *sati* is contested.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-caminho-e-onde-a-meditacao-entra-nele}}

**muditā**
:   Joy at another's good fortune, the opposite of envy; Sujato translates it as "rejoicing." One of the four *brahmavihāras*.\
    → chapter {{cap:bondade-e-compaixao-como-treino}}

## N

**near enemy**
:   In the *Visuddhimagga*, the state that resembles one of the sublime abodes and passes itself off as it: attachment for kindness, sorrow for compassion. The far enemy is the obvious opposite, such as ill will for kindness and cruelty for compassion.\
    → chapter {{cap:bondade-e-compaixao-como-treino}}

**neurogenesis**
:   The production of new neurons. Whether it happens in the adult human, and how much, is contested; there is no basis for saying that meditation creates new neurons.\
    → chapter {{cap:neuroplasticidade-sem-exagero}}

**neuron**
:   A cell specialized in receiving and transmitting signals. It receives messages from other neurons, integrates them and, past a certain threshold, fires an electrical signal.\
    → chapter {{cap:neuroplasticidade-sem-exagero}}

**neuroplasticity**
:   The capacity of the nervous system to change its structure and function in response to use, experience and learning. It is the brain's normal way of working, not a special power switched on by techniques.\
    → chapter {{cap:neuroplasticidade-sem-exagero}}

**nirodha**
:   "Cessation": the third of the Four Noble Truths, the claim that if the thirst ends, the dukkha it produces ends too.\
    → chapter {{cap:as-quatro-nobres-verdades-como-diagnostico}}

**nirvana**
:   In Pali, *nibbāna*: the state in which the cessation of dukkha is complete, a word whose root is associated with the image of a flame going out for lack of fuel. It is not a paradise or a permanent ecstasy, and the schools disagree on how to describe it.\
    → chapter {{cap:as-quatro-nobres-verdades-como-diagnostico}}

**Noble Eightfold Path**
:   The eight factors that, according to the texts, lead to the end of dukkha: right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, attention and concentration. Only the last two, and in part effort, describe what is now called meditation.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-caminho-e-onde-a-meditacao-entra-nele}}

**noninferiority**
:   A kind of trial that does not ask whether one treatment is better than another, but whether it is *not worse* beyond a margin fixed before the study.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-que-a-ciencia-da-meditacao-mostra-e-o-que}}

**noting**
:   A method systematized by the monk Mahasi Sayadaw in the twentieth century: silently naming, with a simple word, what is happening at the moment it happens. The label is a support; knowing the object matters more than the name.\
    → chapter {{cap:observar-sem-agarrar}}

## O

**open monitoring**
:   Observing, without reacting, whatever appears in experience from moment to moment — sounds, sensations, thoughts, emotions — without choosing a fixed object. It is the other basic style of practice, alongside focused attention.\
    → chapter {{cap:atencao-emocao-e-habito}}

## P

**pain modulation**
:   The well-established fact that the pain you feel is not a direct record of what is happening in the tissue: the nervous system amplifies or dampens the signals depending on attention, expectation, beliefs and context. That does not make the pain imaginary.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-corpo-o-sono-e-a-dor}}

**Pali**
:   The ancient language in which the canon of the Theravāda school was preserved.\
    → chapter {{cap:quem-foi-o-buda-e-quem-ele-nao-foi}}

**paññā**
:   Wisdom, discernment: the training of seeing things as the texts say they are — impermanent, unsatisfactory, without a fixed self. One of the three trainings; it includes right view and right intention.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-caminho-e-onde-a-meditacao-entra-nele}}

**perseverative cognition hypothesis**
:   The proposal of Brosschot, Gerin and Thayer (2006): worry and rumination prolong the body's activation before and after a stressful event. The authors themselves call the support preliminary, because much of the data is correlational.\
    → chapter {{cap:estresse-o-alarme-que-nao-desliga}}

**placebo**
:   In medicine, a treatment with no active ingredient that produces real improvement because the person expects to improve. By extension, all the part of a benefit that comes from expectation and context rather than from the ingredient being tested.\
    → chapter {{cap:como-saber-se-algo-funciona}}

**polyvagal theory**
:   Stephen Porges's theory that the vagus nerve has a "ventral" branch, linked to safety and social connection, and a "dorsal" one, linked to paralysis and shutdown. It is popular, but its physiological premises are rejected by most specialists.\
    → chapter {{cap:estresse-o-alarme-que-nao-desliga}}

**publication bias**
:   The tendency of studies with positive results to be published more than those that find nothing. It makes the published literature look more optimistic than reality, and a meta-analysis built on it inherits the optimism.\
    → chapter {{cap:como-saber-se-algo-funciona}}

## R

**randomized controlled trial**
:   A study that compares a group receiving the practice with a control group, deciding by lot who goes into each. It is the most reliable design for answering "does this cause that?"\
    → chapter {{cap:como-saber-se-algo-funciona}}

**reinforcement learning**
:   The process by which a behavior followed by a reward tends to be repeated, and a behavior followed by something bad tends to be avoided. Reinforcement is stronger when there is surprise: dopamine neurons seem to signal the difference between the expected reward and the one received.\
    → chapter {{cap:atencao-emocao-e-habito}}

**relapse**
:   The return of depressive symptoms after a person has improved.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-que-a-ciencia-da-meditacao-mostra-e-o-que}}

**replication**
:   Another group of researchers repeats a study, preferably with more people, and sees whether the result holds. A finding that has appeared only once is a lead, not a fact.\
    → chapter {{cap:como-saber-se-algo-funciona}}

**retreat**
:   A period away from your routine devoted to practice, from a morning to several weeks. An **intensive retreat** lasts several days, sometimes weeks or months, given over almost entirely to formal practice, many hours a day and usually in silence; having been on retreat is associated with more unpleasant experiences.\
    → chapters {{cap:quando-a-meditacao-da-errado}} and {{cap:uma-pratica-para-a-vida}}

**reverse inference**
:   Deducing a mental state from the activation of a brain region: "the amygdala lit up, so the person felt fear." It is not logically valid, and it gives some information only when the region is selective, which the regions in the headlines rarely are.\
    → chapter {{cap:como-saber-se-algo-funciona}}

**rumination**
:   Repetitive, passive thinking about your own distress, its causes and its consequences. It is associated with depression and anxiety, and it is a good modern example of the second arrow.\
    → chapter {{cap:a-mente-que-divaga}}

## S

**samādhi**
:   Concentration or, in Sujato's translation, "immersion": the training of the mind — stability, focus, unification. One of the three trainings; it includes right effort, right attention and right concentration.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-caminho-e-onde-a-meditacao-entra-nele}}

**samatha**
:   Calm, serenity: the development of the mind's stability, linked in the texts to the states of meditative absorption (*jhāna*). In the suttas, it complements *vipassanā*.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-caminho-e-onde-a-meditacao-entra-nele}}

**Sangha**
:   "Community"; in traditional usage, the monastic community. With the Buddha and the Dharma, it makes up the Three Refuges. In practice, for a beginner, it is a group you meditate with regularly.\
    → chapters {{cap:quem-foi-o-buda-e-quem-ele-nao-foi}} and {{cap:uma-pratica-para-a-vida}}

**sati**
:   A Pali term related to the verb *sarati*, "to remember": the quality that keeps something in view in the mind. In the texts, it involves discerning the moral value of what arises; in English, it was translated as *mindfulness*.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-caminho-e-onde-a-meditacao-entra-nele}}

**satipaṭṭhāna**
:   "Presence of mindfulness" (*sati* plus *upaṭṭhāna*, "being present"): the practice described in the *Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta* (MN 10), with four fields of observation and liberation as its goal. In English, the term usually appears as the "foundations of mindfulness."\
    → chapter {{cap:o-caminho-e-onde-a-meditacao-entra-nele}}

**second arrow**
:   An image from the *Sallatha Sutta*: struck by pain, the ordinary person feels two pains — the physical one, which is the first arrow, and the layer of resistance, distress and lament added on top. The first is unavoidable; the second is a response, and responses can be trained.\
    → chapter {{cap:as-quatro-nobres-verdades-como-diagnostico}}

**selection bias**
:   The central problem of studies of long-term practitioners: someone who has meditated for twenty years was not assigned by lot to meditate, and may have been different from the start. Comparing such people with people who have never meditated shows that the groups differ, not that meditation caused the difference.\
    → chapter {{cap:uma-pratica-para-a-vida}}

**self-model**
:   In the philosopher Thomas Metzinger's theory, the representation the brain builds of its own organism, which we live not as a model but as reality itself. The conclusion that there is therefore "no self" is a philosophical thesis, not a laboratory result.\
    → chapter {{cap:impermanencia-e-nao-eu}}

**sīla**
:   Ethics, conduct: the training of what you do and say. One of the three trainings; it includes right speech, right action and right livelihood.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-caminho-e-onde-a-meditacao-entra-nele}}

**social-evaluative threat**
:   The possibility of being judged negatively by others. Combined with a lack of control over the outcome, it produced the largest cortisol responses in laboratory studies, with no physical danger at all.\
    → chapter {{cap:estresse-o-alarme-que-nao-desliga}}

**stress response**
:   The set of changes the body makes when it detects a threat: the heart speeds up, breathing changes, fuel goes to the muscles and functions that can wait are set aside. It uses two routes, the autonomic nervous system and the HPA axis.\
    → chapter {{cap:estresse-o-alarme-que-nao-desliga}}

**sutta**
:   A discourse attributed to the Buddha or to a close disciple. The suttas of the Pali canon are cited by the name of the collection and a number, such as SN 56.11 or MN 10.\
    → chapter {{cap:quem-foi-o-buda-e-quem-ele-nao-foi}}

**synapse**
:   The point where one neuron passes its message to another. Its strength changes with use, and most learning happens at that scale.\
    → chapter {{cap:neuroplasticidade-sem-exagero}}

## T

**taṇhā**
:   "Thirst": the urgency for experience to be different right now, not simple wanting. The text describes it in three forms — thirst for sensual pleasures, for continued existence and for nonexistence — and names it as the cause of dukkha.\
    → chapter {{cap:as-quatro-nobres-verdades-como-diagnostico}}

**teacher**
:   Anyone with training and experience who follows your practice: a certified MBSR or MBCT instructor, an authorized teacher within a Buddhist tradition, a psychologist trained in mindfulness-based interventions.\
    → chapter {{cap:uma-pratica-para-a-vida}}

**Theravāda**
:   The school that kept the suttas in Pali, and the source of most of the texts quoted in this book. It predominates in Thailand, Myanmar, Sri Lanka, Laos and Cambodia.\
    → chapter {{cap:quem-foi-o-buda-e-quem-ele-nao-foi}}

**Three Refuges**
:   The Buddha, the Dharma and the Sangha, also called the Three Jewels.\
    → chapter {{cap:quem-foi-o-buda-e-quem-ele-nao-foi}}

**transfer**
:   The carrying over of a gain trained on one task to other tasks. Whether meditation's attention training transfers to daily life has not yet been well shown.\
    → chapter {{cap:atencao-emocao-e-habito}}

## U

**upekkhā**
:   Equanimity directed toward beings: wishing all of them well without favoring some and excluding others. One of the four *brahmavihāras*.\
    → chapter {{cap:bondade-e-compaixao-como-treino}}

## V

**Vajrayāna**
:   The branch of Buddhism concentrated in Tibet and the surrounding regions, which is why it is also called Tibetan Buddhism. Some scholars count it as part of the Mahāyāna rather than as a third branch.\
    → chapter {{cap:quem-foi-o-buda-e-quem-ele-nao-foi}}

**vedanā**
:   Feeling: the tone of each experience, pleasant, unpleasant or neutral. It is not emotion; it is the fastest layer of reaction, before liking or disliking in words. It is the second of the five aggregates and the second of the four foundations of mindfulness.\
    → chapters {{cap:impermanencia-e-nao-eu}} and {{cap:observar-sem-agarrar}}

**vipassanā**
:   Penetrating vision, *insight*: the development of direct understanding of impermanence, unsatisfactoriness and not-self. In the suttas, it complements *samatha*.\
    → chapter {{cap:o-caminho-e-onde-a-meditacao-entra-nele}}

**Visuddhimagga**
:   "The Path of Purification": the meditation manual of the Theravāda school, written in Pali in Sri Lanka by the monk Buddhaghosa in the fifth century. It shows how the tradition interpreted the suttas; breath counting and the near enemies come from it.\
    → chapter {{cap:atencao-na-respiracao}}

## W

**waitlist**
:   The weakest control, and the most common in meditation research: the control group does nothing during the study and gets the course afterward. It rules out "time passed," but not expectation, the welcoming group or the attentive instructor.\
    → chapter {{cap:como-saber-se-algo-funciona}}

## Z

**Zen**
:   A Mahāyāna school whose name comes from the Chinese *Chán*, which in turn transcribes the Sanskrit *dhyāna*, "meditation." Being the school "of meditation" does not make it a technique: Zen has revered masters and a vast literature of its own.\
    → chapter {{cap:quem-foi-o-buda-e-quem-ele-nao-foi}}
