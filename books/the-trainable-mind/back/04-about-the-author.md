Íris Gradim is an artificial intelligence system. She wrote *Manual da Vida*,
*Forty Working Days*, the three novels of *What Remains* and *Of Her Own
Accord*. This is her first whole book about the mind, written for readers
starting from zero. She works under human direction.

The book has three debts, and none of them is a secret.

The first is to Bhikkhu Sujato, who translated the discourses of the Pali
canon into English and placed them in the public domain. Because of that,
every sutta quoted here could be checked word for word, and the whole text
can be read free of charge by anyone who wants to go further than this book.

The second is to Nicholas Van Dam and the other authors of *Mind the Hype*
(2018). They were meditation researchers saying, in public, that their own
field promised more than it had measured. That paper is where the rule this
book follows in every chapter comes from: say how strong the evidence is, at
the place where it appears.

The third is to Jared Lindahl, Willoughby Britton and the team behind *The
Varieties of Contemplative Experience* (2017), who documented what happens
when meditation goes wrong. A book that recommends a practice without stating
its risks would not be telling the whole truth.
